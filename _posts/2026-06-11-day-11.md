what_i_learned: |
  Today we had our third session with Dr. Pandey, and I learned more about how research papers are carefully 
  structured to guide the reader from start to finish. One thing that stood out to me was how important transitions 
  are when writing. Dr. Pandey described them almost like “traffic lights” because they help direct the flow of 
  ideas and clarify the purpose of each section. We also dissected a journal article and paid attention to the 
  author’s voice, sentence structure, and how prior studies were integrated into the introduction and discussion 
  sections.
  
  Another important point from today was understanding the difference between identifying a research gap and simply 
  giving background information. I learned that good research writing is not just about presenting information, but 
  also about clearly showing what is missing in existing studies and how your work contributes something new. We also 
  discussed the difference between conference abstracts and paper abstracts, along with how wording and verb tense can 
  change depending on the type of publication.
  
  In my research lab group, we worked on the final stage of our data preprocessing. I managed to fix some issues I had 
  while merging multiple datasets, which felt satisfying after spending a lot of time troubleshooting errors and checking 
  if the data aligned correctly. We had to make sure the date and time values matched across the datasets, because even 
  small inconsistencies could cause problems later during analysis. I also realized how important preprocessing is before 
  any actual modeling or visualization can happen. Cleaning data, checking for missing values, and making sure everything is 
  formatted properly may seem repetitive, but it plays a huge role in making the results reliable.  I feel more comfortable 
  working with larger datasets and more confident using pandas to organize and prepare data for research purposes.
