 Today, I continued refining the methodology section of our research paper based on feedback from my graduate mentor. Rather than adding new content, the focus was on improving the overall organization and flow of the section to make it easier for readers to follow. We revisited the order in which concepts such as dataset creation, temporal synchronization, and data preprocessing were introduced, ensuring that each subsection built naturally upon the previous one. This experience showed me that technical writing is an iterative process, often requiring multiple revisions to improve clarity and coherence.

 During the second half of the day, we participated in a SAIRI Connect fellowship session where we watched the film Mercy. Set in a future where an AI judge determines the outcome of criminal trials, the movie explored the possibilities and ethical challenges of integrating artificial intelligence into high-stakes decision-making. While the storyline was fictional, it raised thought-provoking questions about trust, bias, accountability, privacy, and the extent to which society should rely on AI systems for decisions that have significant human consequences. It was interesting to reflect on the difference between developing AI as a tool to support human decision-making and allowing it to replace human judgment altogether.
