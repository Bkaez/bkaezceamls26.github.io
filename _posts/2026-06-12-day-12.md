  Today was our last virtual session with Dr. Pandey and it helped me better understand how much precision and structure 
  matter in research writing and when prompting AI models. One thing I am grateful to have learned throughout her sessions 
  is the difference between a conference abstract and a paper abstract, especially how wording and tense can change 
  depending on whether results are expected or already completed. I also learned how researchers identify research gaps and 
  organize introductions in a way that clearly explains the purpose of a study.
  
  Another important takeaway was learning how AI tools should be documented and evaluated during the writing process. Instead 
  of accepting every response automatically, we discussed the importance of reviewing outputs critically and keeping track of 
  prompts and revisions. That made me realize how careful and detailed academic work really is.
  
  In my project lab, I presented on Decision Tree Regressor and learned more about how machine learning models make predictions 
  from data patterns. We also explored Random Forest Regressor and how combining multiple decision trees can improve accuracy 
  and stability. Seeing these models connected to our own dataset made the concepts easier to understand and showed how 
  preprocessing and clean data directly affect the quality of results.
