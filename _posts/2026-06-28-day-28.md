what_i_learned: |
 Today was dedicated to strengthening our understanding of deep learning and exploring the different approaches that could be applied to our research project. Instead of jumping straight into model development, we spent time reading and analyzing research papers to better understand how different deep learning models work, why they are used, and which ones would be most appropriate for predicting PM2.5 concentrations.

 One of the main topics we explored was the difference between several neural network architectures, including Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). I learned that ANNs serve as the foundation of many deep learning models and work well with structured tabular data. RNNs are designed to process sequential data by retaining information from previous time steps, while LSTMs improve on RNNs by remembering important information over longer periods and reducing the problem of forgetting earlier inputs. Since PM2.5 data changes over time, these models are commonly used for time-series forecasting. We also looked at CNNs, which are typically used for image and spatial data but can also be adapted for certain time-series applications.
