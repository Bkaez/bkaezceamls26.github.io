 Today marked the opening of the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI 2026, bringing together researchers, educators, industry professionals, and students to discuss how artificial intelligence can be developed and applied responsibly. One of the highlights of the first day was the keynote address delivered by Dr. Juan Gilbert, who shared his work on advancing equitable AI in higher education admissions, particularly in the post-Students for Fair Admissions (SFFA) Supreme Court decision era.

 Dr. Gilbert’s keynote focused on the challenge of creating fair and effective admissions systems while navigating the limitations and biases that can exist in traditional approaches. He introduced Applications Quest (AQ), an AI-driven approach designed to help institutions evaluate applications in a more holistic and equitable manner.

 Unlike many traditional AI systems that rely on supervised learning and historical decisions, AQ uses an unsupervised learning approach. The system applies clustering algorithms to automatically analyze and compare application data, identifying similarities and patterns among applicants without requiring predefined labels. One of the key takeaways from this presentation was the importance of designing AI systems that support human decision-making rather than replace it.

Our project explores how AI can help accelerate complex problem-solving while maintaining human oversight to ensure accuracy, reliability, and ethical implementation.
