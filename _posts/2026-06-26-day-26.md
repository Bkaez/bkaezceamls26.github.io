what_i_learned: |
 Today, we traveled to Washington, D.C. to attend the AWS Summit, which was an exciting experience since it was my first time visiting the nation’s capital. Throughout the day, I attended three workshops and explored the expo hall, where I had the opportunity to network with professionals from a variety of companies.

 The workshop that had the greatest impact on me was "Combat Criminal Networks with AI-Powered Investigative Intelligence". I learned how modern AI technologies such as Generative AI, vector embeddings, semantic search, and knowledge graphs can work together to analyze massive amounts of criminal investigation data. Rather than investigators manually searching through thousands of case files, AI can quickly connect related people, events, locations, and evidence across multiple law enforcement databases. This allows investigators to uncover hidden criminal networks, identify trafficking operations, and make faster, more informed decisions. It was interesting to see how AI is being used in real-world public safety applications instead of just traditional business settings.

 Another session that caught my attention focused on Claude Code and the use of AI agents to assist software developers. The speakers demonstrated how AI can automate repetitive coding tasks, help developers understand large codebases, and accelerate software development while still keeping humans in control of the final decisions. Although this session focused on software engineering, it shared many similarities with the investigative intelligence workshop. Both emphasized using AI to process large volumes of information, recognize patterns that humans might overlook, and support professionals by improving efficiency rather than replacing them.

 Outside the workshops, I spent time in the expo hall speaking with professionals from several organizations. I had great conversations with representatives from Anduril, where I shared my interest in server engineering, and IBM, where I discussed my passion for chip development and hardware engineering. Even though many of the representatives specialized in cloud computing and software, they took the time to connect me with colleagues whose expertise aligned more closely with my career interests. These conversations gave me valuable insight into different career paths and the importance of building professional relationships within the industry.
