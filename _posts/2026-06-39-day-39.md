what_i_learned: |
 Today was a pretty good day, my team and I stepped into the field. With smoke from the Canadian wildfires affecting air quality across Maryland, we had the opportunity to collect real-time PM₂.₅ measurements around campus using an enterprise drone equipped with an air quality monitoring sensor.

 I had the chance to pilot the drone during several flights, which was easily one of the highlights of the day. Rather than relying only on historical satellite and weather datasets, we were able to gather real-time environmental measurements and compare them with the conditions we were actually experiencing. During one of our measurements, we recorded PM₂.₅ concentrations of around 300 µg/m³, a level that reflects hazardous air quality and shows just how much the wildfire smoke is impacting the area.

 Seeing the drone collect environmental data gave me a better appreciation for how different technologies work together in air quality research. While our project uses satellite observations, meteorological data, and artificial intelligence to predict PM₂.₅ concentrations, today's fieldwork demonstrated another important way environmental data can be collected and validated. It was rewarding to see our research connect so closely with a real-world air quality event instead of only working with archived datasets.

 I also spent some time making additional edits to our research paper, refining sections of the paper and incorporating feedback as we continue preparing the final version.
