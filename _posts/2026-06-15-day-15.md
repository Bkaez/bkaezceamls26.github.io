what_i_learned: |
  Today marked the first day of the in-person institute, and the transition from virtual sessions to being on-site was smoother than I expected. It was great to finally meet and interact with other participants and mentors in person after working together virtually for the past 3 weeks.

We started the day with a Data Analytics Workshop led by Dr. Jameel Dason. Although many of the tools and concepts were familiar because we have been using them throughout our research project, the workshop helped reinforce the role of Python libraries in the data analytics process. We reviewed how NumPy supports numerical computing, how pandas is used for loading and cleaning datasets, and how visualization tools such as Matplotlib and Seaborn help uncover patterns in data. We also discussed the importance of building baseline machine learning models and evaluating their performance before moving on to more advanced approaches.

Later, Dr. Valerie Riggs led a workshop on AI bias and ethics. One of the most important lessons I took away was that AI systems are only as good as the data they learn from. Because training data is collected and curated by humans, existing biases can be unintentionally embedded into AI models. We examined examples showing significant differences in facial recognition accuracy across demographic groups and discussed how underrepresentation of certain populations in training datasets can lead to unfair outcomes. This session highlighted the importance of thinking critically about where data comes from and who may be excluded from it when developing AI systems.
