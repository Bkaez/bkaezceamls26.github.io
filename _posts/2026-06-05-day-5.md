what_i_learned: |
  Today in our breakout session, my graduate mentor went deeper into the literature behind our research project 
  and explained the relationship between PM2.5 and Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD). I learned that aerosols are tiny 
  particles floating in the atmosphere that can come from both natural and human-made sources. We talked about 
  how AOD measures how much sunlight is blocked by aerosols in the atmosphere and how satellites are used to 
  detect and measure that data. We also discussed how PM2.5 focuses more on the tiny pollution particles closer 
  to the ground that people breathe in, which is why it is strongly connected to health issues. It was interesting 
  seeing how temperature, humidity, satellite data, and pollution measurements all connect together in climate 
  research and air quality prediction.
  
  The AI Quest session was pretty fun.I learned how AI systems are trained using different types of data, the 
  importance of collecting accurate and safe data, and how privacy and ethics play a major role in AI research. I 
  also learned that AI models analyze patterns in data to make predictions, such as detecting floods or identifying 
  environmental conditions.
  
  In Dr. Mack’s Python 101 session, we reviewed some of the topics from last week and then focused on conditional 
  statements. I learned more about how if, elif, and else statements work and how computers make decisions by 
  following algorithms. We also practiced solving a few problems using Boolean values like true and false, which 
  helped me better understand how decision-making works in programming.
