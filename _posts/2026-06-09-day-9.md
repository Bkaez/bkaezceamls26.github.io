what_i_learned: |
  Today’s session focused on computer vision, and it was honestly one of the most interesting topics we’ve 
  covered so far. During the workshop led by Daniel Onyejekwe, I learned that computer vision is basically 
  teaching computers how to “see” and understand images or videos. We discussed how computers break images 
  down into pixels and then analyze patterns like colors, shapes, and edges to identify objects.
  
  I also learned about image classification, object detection, object tracking, and image segmentation. One 
  of the coolest parts was seeing how object detection can place bounding boxes around objects it recognizes 
  in an image. I was also introduced to tools like OpenCV and YOLOv8, which are used for different real-time 
  computer vision tasks.
  
  Another highlight was using Google’s Teachable Machine to train a simple image model with webcam images. What 
  stood out to me the most today was realizing how much computer vision connects to things we use every day, such 
  as facial recognition, self-driving cars, security systems, and even social media filters. It was interesting 
  seeing how AI models learn from examples and improve based on the data they are given. We also practiced resizing, 
  cropping, and displaying images while exploring how computer vision models process visual information.
