what_i_learned: |
  In today’s research lab my team and I had a session with Dr. Xiaowen Li focused more on the atmospheric science behind our PM2.5 research project. I learned about the different kinds of aerosols and their negative and positive effects on nature. We also discussed why aerosols matter, especially their impact on human health, visibility, cloud formation, and precipitation. Another concept that stood out to me was the concept of using multiple satellites to capture Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD). Dr. Li also introduced how AOD can be measured using sun photometers at the surface and retrieved from satellites using algorithms such as the Dark Target algorithm. We discussed the role of both Low Earth Orbit (LEO) and geostationary satellites in monitoring aerosols and collecting atmospheric data.
  
Later in the day, my graduate mentor and I worked on testing different AI models using our datasets. We trained and evaluated several machine learning models, including XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, Linear Regression, and Ridge Regression. I also learned how model performance is evaluated using metrics such as RMSE and R² to measure prediction accuracy and model fit. Comparing the performance of different models made it easier to see how machine learning can be applied to environmental and atmospheric research
