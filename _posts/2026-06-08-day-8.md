what_i_learned: |
  Today we had another session with Dr. Pandey that continued from her previous workshop on writing research 
  papers for publication. This time, she focused more on titles and visuals, and explained that based on the 
  American standard for technical writing, shorter and clearer sentences are preferred. She emphasized that 
  research writing should be direct and easy to understand and not overly complicated. She also talked about 
  how important a paper’s title is because it is usually the first thing people see, and it can determine 
  whether someone decides to keep reading or not. Another major point she stressed was the importance of 
  visuals such as graphs, charts, and figures because they help communicate information more clearly and make 
  research easier to follow.
  
  Later, during the session with my graduate mentor, I was introduced to another Python library called Plotly, 
  which can be used with pandas for plotting graphs and visualizing data. It is probably my favorite library so 
  far because of how clean and minimalistic it is on the coding part, especially when working with large datasets. 
  We practiced plotting graphs using sample datasets and later presented our graphs while interpreting the 
  information shown in them. That activity made me realize how important it is to create graphs that are not only 
  visually appealing but also meaningful and easy to interpret.
  
  We also spent time going through related works by discussing research articles that we have been reading 
  individually this week. We broke down different sections of the different papers and analyzed how the authors 
  presented their research, visuals, and findings. The things Dr. Pandey taught us earlier about technical writing 
  and presentation definitely helped during this activity because I found myself paying closer attention to how 
  the paper was structured and how clearly the information was communicated.
