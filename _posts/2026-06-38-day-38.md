what_i_learned: |
 Today we had a presentation from Dr. Chris Crawford on next-generation physiological computing, and it was easily one of my favorite sessions because it closely aligned with my interests in hardware, robotics, and intelligent systems. He mainly talked about Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs), which enable communication between the brain and external devices by measuring brain signals. I learned about common signal acquisition methods such as EEG (Electroencephalography) and fNIRS (Functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy), as well as how software processes raw brain signals into meaningful information that can be used for real-world applications.One of the most interesting takeaways was seeing how BCIs are already being applied in areas like healthcare, gaming, and controlling drones and robotic systems. He also discussed different control paradigms like active, passive, and reactive BCIs, and the potential for more modular BCI architectures in the future.
 
 Also had the chance to watch the elevator pitch videos created by the other research teams. It was interesting to see the different ways each group chose to communicate their research, and I picked up several creative storytelling and presentation techniques that I can apply to future projects. I also had the opportunity to review my graduate mentor's research poster and provide feedback before her poster presentation. Seeing the amount of thought that goes into presenting research visually gave me a better appreciation for how important clear communication goes beyond writing a research paper.

 Another thing that connected today's learning to our own project was the weather. Temperatures were extremely high, and the Maryland Department of the Environment reported Code Orange air quality conditions. During our lab discussions, we talked about how events like this reinforce the importance of our PM2.5 prediction research. It was meaningful to connect what we are developing in the lab to a real environmental issue that people are experiencing today.
